Next training session

Semester 1, Week 1 — Foundational Guards (Quattuor Custodie) and Stance Mechanics. Source: Fiore dei Liberi, Il Fior di Battaglia (c. 1409). About 105 minutes at rotating Maryborough / Hervey Bay private grounds.

Full lesson plan · Videos by Federico Malagutti

Session objectives

  1. Correct posture, weight distribution, and footwork for late fourteenth-century unarmoured longsword.
  2. Form and hold Fiore’s four primary guards: Tutta Porta di Ferro, Dritto Finestra, Posta di Donna, and Posta Breve.
  3. Transition with passare and volta stabile while keeping centreline coverage.
  4. Complete ALHF/QLHF gear and safety inspection.

Session outline

  • 0:00

    Admin, attendance & safety

    15 min — digital sign-in, insurance check, weapon and gear inspection.

  • 0:15

    Mobility & footwork

    25 min — warm-up; base stance; passare and volta stabile (unarmed).

  • 0:40

    40 min — four guards, solo practice, then continuous transitions.

  • 1:20

    Partner application

    20 min — cooperative, zero-force, no blade contact in Week 1.

  • 1:40

    Cool-down & debrief

    5 min — stretch, Fiore principle review, homework: 10 minutes daily solo footwork through the four guards.

The four guards

  • Tutta Porta di Ferro

    Low guard: lead foot forward, pommel near the belt, blade diagonally toward the ground. Protects the low line; base for upward parries.

  • Dritto Finestra

    High guard: hilt by the right temple, point threatening the eyes or throat. Covers high lines; primed for descending mandritti.

  • Posta di Donna

    High chambered guard: hilt over the shoulder, blade behind the head. Power generator for heavy descending cuts; practise both sides.

  • Posta Breve

    Mid-line guard: hilt at the waist, point on the centre line. Point control and direct thrust threat with minimal exposure.

Instruction stays with Fiore’s terms and biomechanics, even where a posture resembles a German-tradition guard (e.g. Posta di Donna vs wrath guard).
